What it is
</h2>
<p>I built a <code>SuperCard</code> with <code>variant</code>, <code>interactive</code>, <code>size</code>, <code>tone</code>, and a badge flag before I had three real cards. The props described an imaginary product. The one card I actually rendered was harder to read than a plain <code>&lt;article&gt;</code>.</p>
<p>Duplication is cheaper than the wrong abstraction, especially in a personal app whose UI is still moving. I copy markup until a third use shows me the part that is actually stable. Then I extract that part, not a framework.</p>
<p>I prefer composition. <code>Card</code>, <code>CardHeader</code>, and <code>CardBody</code> can be arranged per screen. A single component with twelve booleans hides the combinations and forces every new case through another prop. Boolean props also interact: <code>interactive</code> plus <code>disabled</code> plus <code>loading</code> is a matrix I did not mean to design.</p>
<p>Early abstraction feels like progress because the file count drops. Late extraction is usually wiser. If I am inventing a prop for a case I do not have, I wait.</p>
</section>

New

<p><strong>Right approach: duplicate until the repeated part is obvious, then compose small pieces</strong></p>
<p><strong>Best case.</strong> The header and body can change independently. A screen without a badge simply does not render one. There is no <code>showBadge={false}</code>.</p>
<p><strong>Worst case.</strong> Extracting after one use, or extracting the wrong seam, so every call site still passes a pile of configuration. I would rather undo that extraction than defend it.</p>

<section class="section" id="when">
<h2>
<svg class="ico" width="14" height="14" viewBox="0 0 14 14" aria-hidden="true" fill="none" stroke="#111" stroke-width="1.25" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"><path d="M3.6 2.4v9.2"/><path d="M3.6 6.4h4.35"/><path d="M7.95 6.4 11.15 3.5"/><path d="M7.95 6.4 11.15 9.3"/></svg>
When to use which
</h2>
<p>Copy the markup for the first and second real use. On the third, extract the part that was actually identical. Prefer children and small components over boolean modes. Add a prop when a call site needs to vary something that composition cannot say.</p>
<div class="when">
<div class="when-row"><span>First or second similar UI</span><span class="when-choice">duplicate it</span></div>
<div class="when-row"><span>Third use with a stable seam</span><span class="when-choice">extract that seam</span></div>
<div class="when-row"><span>Optional sections</span><span class="when-choice">compose them</span></div>
<div class="when-row"><span>A matrix of boolean flags</span><span class="when-choice">split the component</span></div>
</div>
</section>
